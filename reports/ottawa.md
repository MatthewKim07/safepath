# Cross-city test: Ottawa (CP9)

Toronto-trained models scored on Ottawa intersections they never saw, with no Ottawa crash labels in any training set. Compared with Ottawa-native baselines and an Ottawa-trained upper bound. Ranking metrics are primary because the two cities record collisions differently; calibration is shown raw and after one level scalar per model fit on the prior year only. Everything below is regenerated by `python -m safepath ottawa`.

Model groups and the Ottawa data their inputs use (no group trains on Ottawa crash labels except C and D):

- **(A)** Toronto-trained, no Ottawa crash labels. Road-only LightGBM, road-only MLP and the count-free SPF use OSM only; the SPF with Ottawa AADT and road-only GraphSAGE (edge AADT) also use Ottawa traffic counts for exposure.
- **(B)** Toronto-trained, Ottawa crash history as input features plus Ottawa counts for exposure; still no Ottawa training labels.
- **(C)** Ottawa-native baselines from Ottawa crash labels (2016-2019) or history.
- **(D)** LightGBM + AADT trained on Ottawa 2016-2019 labels: the upper bound.

## Study area and graph

City of Ottawa Official Plan urban boundary (`https://maps.ottawa.ca/arcgis/rest/services/Planning/MapServer/13`), 643 km² of the 2,790 km² amalgamated city (Toronto: 630 km²). 122,623 non-freeway crashes inside, 13,019 outside (rural, out of scope). OSM drive graph 19,013 nodes / 49,379 edges; consolidation at 10 m (as Toronto) gives **15,276 intersections** (Toronto 22,435).

## Noise ceiling and sparsity

Thinning is the clean test of sparsity. The Poisson ceiling (a lower bound here, see the conclusions): if a model's expected counts were the true ones, the Spearman a perfect model reaches against one simulated year (200 draws). Expected counts: Ottawa, LightGBM + AADT trained on Ottawa; Toronto, its own LightGBM + AADT; both scaled to the observed total. Thinning keeps each Toronto crash with probability Ottawa rate / Toronto rate (50 draws). Ceilings are approximate: true risk is more spread out than any model's estimate, so the real ceiling is higher.

## Conclusions

Two views of the same test year (2024), every difference with a paired 95% bootstrap CI over intersections. Spearman is dominated by ties here (83% of intersection-years have no crash), so hotspot recall@5% and PR-AUC are co-primary.

- **On Spearman, nothing beats Ottawa's own prev-3-year mean (0.526).** Best transferred model ((B) EB (Toronto SPF + Ottawa history)) minus it: -0.077 [-0.087, -0.066] (worse); the Ottawa-trained upper bound minus it: -0.038 [-0.050, -0.027] (worse).
- **On hotspot recall, Toronto models fed Ottawa's crash history match the Ottawa-trained upper bound.** Recall@5% minus the upper bound for each group-B model: LightGBM + AADT -0.001 [-0.005, +0.002] (no different); LightGBM + AADT + neighbours +0.000 [-0.006, +0.003] (no different); EB (Toronto SPF + Ottawa history) +0.001 [-0.005, +0.006] (no different); GraphSAGE with history -0.002 [-0.008, +0.001] (no different) - every CI contains zero and lies within ±0.008. On PR-AUC they trail it (-0.051 to -0.026, all CIs below zero), so the upper bound ranks the whole list better while the top 5% is the same.
- **EB vs persistence** is the same split: Spearman -0.055 [-0.067, -0.043] (worse), recall@5% +0.009 [+0.001, +0.016] (better), PR-AUC +0.046 [+0.037, +0.055] (better).
- **With no Ottawa data at all, the count-free SPF roughly doubles the road-class baseline's hotspot recall**: 0.619 vs 0.288 (x2.1; difference +0.331 [+0.295, +0.369] (better); PR-AUC +0.189 [+0.172, +0.206] (better)). On Spearman it is below the class means (-0.035 [-0.052, -0.018] (worse)): the class mean ties whole classes, which Spearman rewards on mostly-zero data, but it cannot pick out the busy few. The best road-only model on validation Spearman, (A) MLP, road features only, shows the same pattern: Spearman -0.032 [-0.047, -0.016] (worse), recall@5% +0.229 [+0.188, +0.276] (better).
- **Why Ottawa's numbers are lower than Toronto's: sparsity.** Ottawa has 0.57 crashes per intersection-year against Toronto's 2.35. Thinning Toronto's own 2025 crashes to Ottawa's rate drops Toronto's LightGBM + AADT from 0.744 to 0.584 Spearman with nothing else changed. Compare models within a city, never Ottawa's Spearman with Toronto's.
- **Does the graph help in a new city?** Not clearly. Road-only GraphSAGE minus LightGBM: Spearman +0.034 [+0.024, +0.043] (better), recall@5% -0.030 [-0.050, -0.011] (worse); minus the graph-free MLP: Spearman -0.014 [-0.022, -0.004] (worse), recall@5% +0.039 [+0.008, +0.058] (better).
- **Volumes transfer badly**: Toronto's AADT model on Ottawa counts R² 0.57 (raw betweenness 0.64), over-predicting x1.8; Ottawa class medians 0.72; an Ottawa-trained model 0.85. Exposure for the count-using models is Ottawa's (Ottawa-trained intersection model blended with counts).
- **Feature shift** (|SMD| > 0.2): entering_aadt -0.38, max_maxspeed_kph +0.31, max_lanes -0.50, n_pois_100m -0.25, streetcar_20m -0.29, dist_motorway_m +0.50. Crashes per million entering vehicles: Ottawa 0.24, Toronto 0.61; part of that is geocoding (TPS puts ~96% of crashes on intersections, Ottawa ~75%).
- **Verdict:** for finding the top hotspots, Toronto's models transfer: with Ottawa history they find as many of the next year's crashes in their top 5% as a model trained on Ottawa, and with no Ottawa data at all the count-free SPF finds about twice what a road-class lookup does. On Spearman, Ottawa's own three-year average is still best and no Toronto model beats it (on PR-AUC the Ottawa-trained model is ahead of that average, +0.109 [+0.102, +0.116] (better)).

## Limitations

- **2024 recording change.** Non-reportable records jump from under 100 a year to 2,358 (dropped), and the severe share falls from 16-20% to 8.8% with fatal and major-injury crashes steady: minor-injury crashes are recorded differently. The any-crash target looks consistent (2024 total in line with 2019), but the severe target is not compared and the test-year level may still be affected.
- **2023 is unpublished.** Test 2024's 'previous year' is 2022, so persistence-style features are a year staler than in Toronto, and the level scalar comes from 2022.
- **Different geocoding.** TPS puts almost every crash on an intersection; Ottawa keeps mid-block crashes mid-block. Toronto's intersection labels therefore include crashes Ottawa's do not, which shifts levels and what 'intersection risk' means.
- **Exposure is Ottawa-derived for most models.** Only road-only LightGBM, road-only MLP and the count-free SPF use no Ottawa data at all. Segment AADT is Toronto's segment model with a betweenness variant and level correction chosen on Ottawa's intersection counts; Ottawa's mid-block counts were not used to validate it.
- **Static features are today's OSM**, for every year, in both cities.
- **GNN training on MPS is not bit-for-bit reproducible**; seed-averaged results move by about 0.0001 Spearman between runs.
